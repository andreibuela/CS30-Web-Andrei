THE IMPACT OF MUSIC GENRES ON ACADEMIC PRODUCTIVITY

This article was prepared for CS30-8P Websites and Online Communication for Mapúa University students of BS Technical Communication. It presents research findings on how music genres influenced essay-writing productivity under the 14-Week Trimestral System.

ACADEMIC CONTEXT

Mapúa University operated on a compressed 14-Week Trimestral System that required students to produce large volumes of work in short bursts. Essay writing was one of the most cognitively demanding tasks, requiring idea generation, logical structuring, vocabulary retrieval, and syntactic formulation under severe time pressure.

Students relied on music as a coping mechanism. The Arousal-Mood Hypothesis suggested music improved performance by regulating mood and arousal, while Cognitive Load Theory warned that music, especially with lyrics, competed for limited working memory resources.

GENRE PREFERENCES

Survey data revealed strong preferences for Pop, OPM, and Rock. Students favored these over Classical or Lo-fi, showing that emotional regulation and familiarity outweighed concerns about interference.

  • Pop provided rhythm and predictability.
  • OPM reinforced cultural identity.
  • Rock offered intensity and energy.

LYRICS VS FOCUS

The “Irrelevant Speech Effect” explained why lyrics interfered with verbal tasks. Yet, students reported that lyrics had not hindered them. Instead, preferred genres provided emotional uplift, reduced stress, and sustained focus.

  1. Familiar lyrics faded into the background.
  2. Lyrics provided companionship during solitary writing.
  3. Adaptation transformed interference into resilience.

PRODUCTIVITY TRADE-OFFS

Research showed that music reduced words-per-minute output compared to silence. However, productivity was not only about speed—it was also about endurance and quality. Music elevated mood, reduced stress, and increased willingness to stay engaged with tasks.

"Students explained that music helped them push through late-night writing sessions, preventing burnout and maintaining a sense of control."

INDIVIDUAL DIFFERENCES

Not all students responded to music the same way. Personality and baseline arousal levels played a major role. Introverts were more easily overstimulated and often found music distracting, while extraverts required stimulation to reach optimal focus.

Introverts
Benefited from silence or Lo-fi, which reduced overstimulation.
Extraverts
Required Pop or Rock to elevate arousal and sustain focus.

LO-FI AND ACOUSTIC CONTROL

Lo-fi music emerged as a middle ground between silence and lyrical distraction. Characterized by steady beats and absence of vocals, Lo-fi created a predictable, unobtrusive soundscape. It masked disruptive environmental noise while providing mood regulation benefits.

IMPLICATIONS

For Mapúa undergraduates, music was not just background—it was a survival strategy. Understanding how different genres affected productivity allowed students to make informed choices about their study playlists.

CONCLUSION

The study validated the Arousal-Mood Hypothesis. Preferred genres, even those with lyrics, enhanced productivity by regulating mood and sustaining motivation. While Cognitive Load Theory warned of interference, the lived experience of students showed that emotional benefits outweighed cognitive costs.
